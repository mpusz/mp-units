    Report from the Búzios 2026 ISO C++ Committee meeting¶

    The Búzios 2026 ISO C++ Committee meeting in Brazil has just finished. C++26 failed its DIS ballot, so most of the week went to the national body comments, and many of them were about contracts. Quantities and units still got about a day in SG6 (Numerics). Below I describe what happened there, and I ask for your opinion on a change to the multiply syntax that two of my papers may force on the library.

    C++26 and contracts¶

    The Draft International Standard (DIS) is the document the national bodies vote on before a new C++ standard is published. This time they voted it down, and a large share of their comments asked for contracts to be removed or changed. The plenary spent Monday and Tuesday on what to do about it.

    Contracts stay in the revised DIS. A poll to remove them didn't reach consensus (41 for, 90 against, 20 abstaining). To answer the comments, the committee added an always-enforced label (P4451R2), so that a contract assertion can be marked to be enforced in every build. The revised DIS was approved for a new ballot (111 for, 9 against, 8 abstaining), which takes eight weeks. If it passes, C++26 ships with contracts. I describe the dispute and this week's outcome in more detail in my C++26 contracts series on the Train IT blog, most recently in C++26 contracts after the Búzios meeting.

    mp-units already uses C++26 contracts. With the contracts=std Conan option (or MP_UNITS_API_CONTRACTS=STD in CMake), the library checks its preconditions and postconditions with C++26 contract assertions. This support is experimental, requires GCC 16 with -fcontracts, and ships in 2.6.0. The Búzios polls don't affect it. The always-enforced label is an addition, and the syntax that mp-units uses didn't change.

    Another DIS change touches this library. "Pragmatic approach to standard structural types" (P3861R1) makes std::string_view, std::span, std::tuple, std::optional, and std::variant structural types, so they can be used as template arguments. std::variant was added to the paper during this meeting. The paper does it by compiler magic. The library wording names these types as structural, a status that no user-defined type with private members can get.

    I don't like this approach. Special-casing a few standard types isn't a generic solution, and library authors still have no easier way to make their own types structural. In mp-units, quantity still has to keep its value in a public data member (numerical_value_is_an_implementation_detail_) to be usable as a template argument. The same applies to our users. If quantities are to be used as template arguments, a custom representation type has to be a structural type too.

    What was voted in¶

    Besides the DIS work, the closing plenary also took the usual polls for the C++29 working draft. Here are the most important ones.

    Core Language (CWG)¶

    • "break label; and continue label;" (P3568R4): a labeled loop can be left or continued from inside a nested loop without a flag variable or a goto.
    • "Deduced return types for defaulted operator functions" (P2952R5): a defaulted operator can be declared with a placeholder return type (auto, or auto& for an assignment), as operator<=> already could.
    • "Oo... adding a coherent character sequence to begin octal-literals" (P0085R5): a 0o prefix for octal literals, so an octal number no longer has to be spelled with a leading 0.
    • "Addenda to the Undefined Behavior and IFNDR Annexes" (P4284R2): extends the catalog of undefined behavior and IFNDR adopted in Brno.

    Library (LWG)¶

    • "Carry-less product: std::clmul" (P3642R8): carry-less multiplication, a building block of fast CRC and of GCM, as a standard function.
    • "any_view" (P3411R7): a type-erased view.
    • "views::slice" (P3216R6): a view of a sub-range given by two indices.
    • "Hazard Pointer Synchronous Reclamation" (P3427R9): extends the hazard pointers added in C++26.
    • "get()ing the pointer from optional" (P4189R0).

    Quantities and units progress¶

    My papers were planned for LEWG this week, but the DIS work took the room, and they were dropped from the agenda. SG6 was first cut from a day and a half to half a day. In the end it met for the full day and a half (Thursday afternoon and all of Friday), and quantities and units took most of it.

    SG6 (Numerics): absolute quantities¶

    I presented R3 of P4185, the paper that adds absolute quantities as a third abstraction next to deltas and points. You can read the motivation in Introducing Absolute Quantities. This revision came with implementation experience.

    • Vectors. An absolute quantity is always a scalar. A vector has a zero, but not the ratio scale an amount needs. After the first session, I changed the rules so that a vector quantity such as displacement or force is implicitly a delta, and it no longer has to be wrapped in delta<...>. Writing isq::velocity[m / s](vec) is enough. Multiplying a vector by a bare unit (a vector times N) is ill-formed.
    • Products of deltas. A lot of time went to what multiplying two deltas means. The volume of a box built from three deltas is physically an absolute, yet under the rules of R3 it comes out as a delta. The change of an area isn't the product of the changes of its sides either. Some suggested that the word "delta" is misleading for the cases where the values are really absolute amounts.
    • One value, two abstractions. It was suggested that some results are both an absolute and a delta at the same time, and that the type system can't always pick the right one. A type that converts either way was discussed, but it would need to support every operator.
    • Generic code. One participant asked how such types compose with generic code that expects plain numbers.

    The feedback was mixed. Some saw the design as an improvement, others doubt it is the right approach at all, and it isn't ready to be forwarded. I was asked for many more examples, each showing the type that comes out of every operation.

    POLL: P4185R3 explores an interesting direction. We encourage more work as we think the design space is not fully explored yet.

    Strongly in Favor In favor Neutral Against Strongly Against
    3 3 1 2 0

    As the next step, I plan to encode the defining equations of ISO 80000 as compile-time tests, each with the expected abstraction of its result, and report how many come out right.

    SG6 (Numerics): non-negative quantity specifications¶

    P4411R1 adds a flag to quantity specifications such as length, mass, or duration that says their absolute values can't be negative. With absolute quantities, creating a negative absolute of such a quantity is then a contract violation. The mp-units master branch (to ship in 2.6.0) already applies the flag to points measured from a natural origin.

    Most of the session went to the cases where the check behaves inconsistently. isq::speed(v) is checked, but the same value created as x * (m / s) isn't, because a unit expression carries no sign. Some definitions hide a sign that the type can't see, like reactive power (Q = U * I * sin(phi)), which is negative for some angles even though the voltage and current amplitudes aren't. One participant asked for a compile-time error, instead of a run-time contract violation, when a value that may be negative is assigned to a non-negative quantity. Another suggested a way to mark an equation as possibly negative.

    POLL: We encourage more work for getting non-negative quantity specs into absolute quantities as this would likely increase consensus on absolute quantities.

    Strongly in Favor In favor Neutral Against Strongly Against
    4 1 1 1 0

    POLL: We encourage more work for getting non-negative quantity specs even if absolute quantities doesn't progress, as this would likely increase consensus on the main quantities and units paper.

    Strongly in Favor In favor Neutral Against Strongly Against
    0 5 2 1 0

    Non-negativity makes most sense together with absolute quantities.

    SG6 (Numerics): conversions between integer representation types¶

    Today the library follows std::chrono::duration, which doesn't compare integer types, so quantity<m, std::int16_t> dist = 100'000 * m; compiles and wraps around to -31072 m. P4408R1 proposes that a conversion between two integer representation types be implicit only when the destination can represent every value of the source.

    The chair asked for one principle for every arithmetic conversion, either the full value-preserving rule (as std::simd does), which would also make int to float explicit, or no check beyond what the representation type itself does. Another participant suggested allowing no implicit conversion at all. There was also a concern that a rule depending on the widths of the types on a given platform makes correctness non-portable, as long differs between targets.

    POLL: Implicit conversions between builtin representation types must be value-preserving (see P2509 or [simd]).

    Strongly in Favor In favor Neutral Against Strongly Against
    6 2 0 0 0

    SG6 chose the value-preserving rule. The next revision of P4408 will use the trait from P2509, the same rule that std::simd follows, so it covers more than conversions between integer types. For example, a conversion from int to float will no longer be implicit either. I plan to implement it in V3. This is a breaking change: code that relies on such implicit conversions will need an explicit value_cast.

    SG6 (Numerics): value conversions with a rounding policy¶

    At the end of Friday, I showed how value conversions changed since P3045 left SG6. A conversion that can't be exact now takes a rounding policy, as in q.in(km, rounded_up), instead of a separate forcing function. The chair saw it as an improvement, said it doesn't need to come back to SG6, and initially found the name rounded unclear. It helped when I explained that it behaves like std::chrono::round. Another participant suggested also accepting std::float_round_style.

    The multiply syntax¶

    In mp-units today, 42 * km / h is two operations: (42 * km) / h. The library allows multiplying and dividing a quantity by a unit, so the units of a derived quantity can be written without parentheses. In the prototype that implements both P4185 and P4411, the first multiplication already creates an absolute quantity and checks its sign before the second unit is applied. The order of the units then decides whether a mistake is caught:

    #include <mp-units/systems/si.h>

using namespace mp_units;
using namespace mp_units::si::unit_symbols;

quantity q1 = -1. * A * s;    // OK: (-1. * A) is a current, which may be negative
quantity q2 = -1. * s * A;    // contract violation: (-1. * s) is a negative duration
quantity q3 = -1. * (A * s);  // OK: one unit, no intermediate quantity

quantity v1 = -1. * m / s;    // contract violation: (-1. * m) is a negative length
quantity v2 = -1. * (m / s);  // unchecked: the kind of m / s carries no sign

    q1 and q2 hold the same value of the same type, but only one of them is checked. The fix I see is to allow the multiply syntax only for number * unit, so that a derived unit always needs parentheses:

    quantity speed = 42 * (km / h);  // OK
quantity bad = 42 * km / h;      // would no longer compile

    This came up in SG6 during the P4411 session, and two participants said they prefer the parentheses or could live with them. Still, it changes a lot of existing code. mp-units required these parentheses once before, and we dropped the rule because it proved too verbose and confusing for users. The parentheses would also remove a surprise we have today, where 60 * km / 2 * h gives 30 km·h instead of 30 km/h.

    Would you accept writing 42 * (km / h) everywhere to get checks that don't depend on the order of the units, or do you see a better way? Please let me know in the comments below.

    What is still ahead¶

    None of the three papers discussed this week was forwarded. Each of them needs another revision and another session in SG6. Only then can it go to LEWG, together with the main paper, P3045, which still needs its design review there and its wording review in LWG.

    Two more papers are already in the mailing and weren't discussed yet: P4213 with the minimal set of SI definitions, and P4412 with range-validated quantity points. Eight more are drafted and wait for a future mailing. They cover comparing quantities against zero, component vectors of vector quantities, integer division, digit grouping and composing formatters in std::format, vector, tensor, and complex operations, runtime frame projections, and the wording for the whole library. Papers on mathematical functions, and probably on more systems of units, still have to be written.

    The library needs work too. V3, which implements the absolute quantities described above, has to be finished and released. Then it needs production experience.

    Supporting the project¶

    I am grateful to the C++ Alliance for covering my travel to Búzios. Thanks to that, I could present three papers in SG6 and get the feedback described above. But finishing everything listed in the previous section will take hundreds or even thousands of hours. I run a one-person company, and paying for that time on my own isn't easy, so I am asking for your support.

    If your company depends on mp-units or wants quantities and units in the C++ standard, please get in touch to discuss a sponsorship. A company can also book my Domain Modeling with the C++ Type System workshop for its team, which helps fund this work. Individuals can support the project through GitHub Sponsors.
